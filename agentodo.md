# Agent TODO — Classical Arabic Grammar Learning Web App
## Based on J. A. Haywood & H. M. Nahmad, *A New Arabic Grammar*

> **Project status:** Product and curriculum specification  
> **Deployment target:** GitHub Pages  
> **Primary audience:** English-speaking learners studying Classical Arabic grammar for reading the Qur’an and classical Arabic texts  
> **Working principle:** Teach the grammar through short, interactive learning loops—not by turning the textbook into a page-by-page digital copy.

---

# 1. Product Vision

Build a lightweight, browser-based Arabic grammar learning application that reorganizes the curriculum of J. A. Haywood and H. M. Nahmad’s *A New Arabic Grammar* into a modern, self-paced learning experience.

The supplied curriculum is specifically oriented toward **Classical Arabic, Qur’anic syntax, morphology (*ṣarf*), syntax (*naḥw*), grammatical inflection (*iʿrāb*), and classical-text reading**. It excludes modern conversation, journalism, and spoken dialect lessons. Keep that distinction clear in the product.

The application should help learners move through this cycle:

1. **Understand** a grammar concept in plain English.
2. **See** the Arabic form, fully vowelled where appropriate.
3. **Recognise** the pattern in examples.
4. **Practise** through small interactive exercises.
5. **Parse** the sentence using grammatical terminology.
6. **Translate** only after understanding the grammatical structure.
7. **Review** weak areas using spaced repetition.

The goal is not to replace the textbook or a qualified teacher. It is to make the curriculum easier to navigate, practise, review, and apply to authentic Arabic texts.

## Learning outcomes

By completing the curriculum, a learner should progressively be able to:

- Read Arabic script and recognise the main orthographic and phonetic signs.
- Identify nominal and verbal sentences.
- Recognise noun gender, number, definiteness, and case.
- Understand the three-case system and common *iʿrāb* markers.
- Identify roots, patterns, derived verb forms, and irregular verb classes.
- Recognise particles that govern the indicative, subjunctive, and jussive moods.
- Parse common nominal and verbal sentence structures.
- Identify major accusative functions, relative clauses, conditionals, exceptions, and other advanced structures.
- Use root-based dictionaries more effectively.
- Read selected Qur’anic and classical passages with a structured grammatical method.

# 2. Curriculum Summary

The supplied curriculum is arranged as **26 weeks in three modules**. The week labels are a suggested learning path, not a requirement that every learner finish a week in seven days.

## Module 1 — Foundations: Script, Nouns, Cases, and Basic Verbs
**Weeks 1–7 | Chapters 1–13**

Purpose: establish the learner’s foundation in Arabic script, nominal syntax, noun inflection, pronouns, and the basic perfect verb.

| Week | Chapters | Main learning content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–2 | Arabic orthography and phonetics; short and long vowels; *sukūn*, *shaddah*, *maddah*; *hamzat al-qaṭʿ* and *hamzat al-waṣl*; solar and lunar letters; the definite article; nominal sentence, *mubtadaʾ* and *khabar*; definiteness and *tanwīn*. |
| 2 | 3–4 | Masculine and feminine nouns; grammatical gender; collective nouns and nouns of unity; interrogative particles; nominative, accusative, and genitive cases; basic case functions and markers. |
| 3 | 5–6 | Singular, dual, and plural; sound masculine and feminine plurals; independent pronouns; triliteral roots; common broken-plural patterns; agreement with non-human broken plurals. |
| 4 | 7–8 | More broken-plural patterns; root-pattern recognition; *iḍāfah* (genitive construct); *muḍāf* and *muḍāf ilayh*; construct chains and their case rules. |
| 5 | 9–10 | Attached pronouns on nouns and prepositions; pronominal suffixes; demonstratives for near and distant reference; demonstrative phrases versus complete nominal sentences. |
| 6 | 11–12 | Adjective–noun agreement in gender, number, case, and definiteness; comparative and superlative forms; Form I perfect verb; conjugation; verbal sentence order and agreement. |
| 7 | 13 | Attached object pronouns on verbs; *kāna wa-akhawātuhā*; *ism kāna* remains nominative and *khabar kāna* becomes accusative; consolidation of Chapters 1–13. |

**Module 1 checkpoint:** Learner can read simple vowelled sentences, identify their core structure, assign basic case endings, recognise common noun forms, and conjugate regular Form I perfect verbs.

## Module 2 — The Verbal System, Moods, and Morphological Forms
**Weeks 8–16 | Primarily Chapters 14–31 and 33 in the supplied outline**

Purpose: understand the imperfect verb, mood governance, derived verb forms, weak and irregular verbs, and root-based dictionary lookup.

| Week | Chapters | Main learning content |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 14–15 | Imperfect verb (*muḍāriʿ*); indicative mood; prefixes; future particles *sa-* and *sawfa*; negation; subjunctive mood and governing particles such as *an*, *lan*, *kay*, *li-*, and *ḥattā*. |
| 9 | 16–18 | Jussive mood and its triggers; *lam* and prohibitive *lā*; conditional particles (introduction); imperative formation; passive perfect and imperfect; *nāʾib al-fāʿil*. |
| 10 | 19–20 | The ten derived triliteral forms; how added morphology can affect meaning; Forms II, III, and IV; conjugation, verbal nouns (*maṣādir*), and active/passive participles. |
| 11 | 21 | Forms V and VI; reflexive, resultative, mutual, and reciprocal meanings; changes in transitivity. |
| 12 | 22 | Forms VII and VIII; passive-like or intransitive patterns; Form VIII root assimilation and recognition of underlying roots. |
| 13 | 23 | Forms IX and X; colours and physical characteristics; seeking/requesting meanings; conjugation and derived participles. |
| 14 | 24–26 | Doubled verbs; *idghām* and uncontracted forms; hamzated verbs; hamza spelling and irregular imperative forms. |
| 15 | 27–28 | Assimilated verbs with an initial weak radical; hollow verbs with a middle weak radical; changes across perfect, imperfect, and jussive forms. |
| 16 | 29–31, 33 | Defective and doubly weak verbs; weak-letter deletion; quadriliteral verbs; root extraction and classical dictionary lookup. |

**Module 2 checkpoint:** Learner can identify the imperfect mood, explain common governing particles, recognise the ten derived forms, identify major irregular verb classes, and retrieve a likely root for dictionary research.

## Module 3 — Advanced Syntax and Classical Reading
**Weeks 17–26 | Chapters 32 and 34–52, plus the literary supplement**

Purpose: combine morphology and syntax to analyse increasingly complex sentences and read authentic classical material.

| Week | Chapters | Main learning content |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | 32, 34 | Special and invariable verbs: *niʿma*, *biʾsa*, *laysa*, *kāda*, and *ʿasā*; relative pronouns; relative clauses; the returning pronoun (*al-ʿāʾid*). |
| 18 | 35 | Conditional sentences; *in*, *idhā*, *law*, and *lawlā*; conditional result clauses and the connector *fa-* (*fāʾ al-jazāʾ*). |
| 19 | 36–37 | Cardinal and ordinal numbers; gender polarity; compound numbers; counted nouns (*tamyīz al-ʿadad*); fractions. |
| 20 | 38–41 | Verbal nouns; nouns of place, time, and instrument; diminutives; *nisbah* adjectives; intensive forms; classical personal-name structures. |
| 21 | 42–44 | Exceptional gender and plural categories; plurals of paucity and multitude; plural of plurals; diptote declension (*al-mamnūʿ min al-ṣarf*). |
| 22 | 45–46 | Accusative functions: direct object, cognate accusative, circumstantial accusative, specification, and vocative; apposition (*al-badal*). |
| 23 | 47–48 | Prepositions and their meanings; adverbial expressions; idiomatic verb–preposition combinations; *inna wa-akhawātuhā* and their case governance. |
| 24 | 49–51 | Conjunction particles and their nuances; oaths; interjections; exception with *illā*, *ghayr*, and *siwā*. |
| 25 | 52 | Classical Arabic prosody (*al-ʿarūḍ*); *sabab*, *watad*, major metres, and rhyme. |
| 26 | Literary supplement | Guided reading and grammatical analysis of selected Qur’anic passages, Sūrat al-Fātiḥah, selected hadith from *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, *Kalīlah wa-Dimnah*, and selections associated with Ibn Baṭṭūṭah. Introduce the use of classical tafsīr, anthologies, and advanced grammar references. |

**Module 3 checkpoint:** Learner can analyse a wider range of classical sentence structures and apply grammar knowledge to guided reading of authentic texts.

# 3. Teaching Method: Modern Learning, Classical Subject Matter

Use modern instructional design while keeping the grammar content faithful to the supplied Classical Arabic scope.

## 3.1 Lesson structure

Each lesson should follow a consistent sequence:

1. **Learning objective** — one measurable outcome.
2. **Recall prompt** — one or two questions from earlier lessons.
3. **Concept explanation** — concise English explanation, avoiding unnecessary jargon at first.
4. **Arabic model** — fully vowelled example, with optional word-by-word reveal.
5. **Rule card** — concise rule, pattern, or declension table.
6. **Guided practice** — learner answers with immediate feedback.
7. **Independent practice** — a small set of questions without hints.
8. **Iʿrāb lab** — identify sentence type, word roles, case/mood, and governing factor.
9. **Translation** — translate after parsing.
10. **Exit check** — 3–5 questions; recommend review or progression.

Keep each lesson focused. Split a large chapter into multiple short lessons rather than presenting a long textbook-like page.

## 3.2 Core practice modes

Implement these reusable exercise types:

- **Recognise:** identify a grammatical form or category.
- **Classify:** select noun/verb/particle, gender, number, case, mood, or verb form.
- **Match:** connect Arabic terms, meanings, patterns, and grammatical roles.
- **Inflect:** choose or enter the correct case/mood ending.
- **Conjugate:** complete a verb paradigm.
- **Build:** arrange words into a valid sentence.
- **Fill the gap:** select the correct form after a governing particle.
- **Root detective:** infer the root and identify added letters.
- **Iʿrāb parsing:** annotate each word’s role and inflection.
- **Translate:** produce a meaning after the structure is understood.
- **Read and analyse:** work through a short, appropriately sourced classical passage.

## 3.3 Feedback rules

Feedback must explain **why** an answer is correct or incorrect.

For a grammar answer, show where relevant:

- Correct answer.
- The rule being tested.
- The governing word or particle (*ʿāmil*).
- The grammatical role.
- The case or mood and its sign.
- A short explanation of the common error.

Do not mark an answer wrong solely because of harmless spelling differences where the exercise is testing grammar. Conversely, do not accept a grammatically incorrect ending merely because the translation seems plausible.

## 3.4 Reading and iʿrāb method

For every suitable sentence, train learners to:

1. Identify whether it is nominal (*jumlah ismiyyah*) or verbal (*jumlah fiʿliyyah*).
2. Identify the core structure:
   - Nominal: *mubtadaʾ* + *khabar*.
   - Verbal: *fiʿl* + *fāʿil* + optional *mafʿūl bihi*.
3. Identify particles and governing factors (*ʿāmil*).
4. Determine each word’s syntactic role.
5. Determine the case or mood and its visible or estimated sign.
6. Read the sentence with the relevant endings.
7. Translate the sentence.

For Qur’anic material, distinguish the grammatical exercise from tafsīr and theological interpretation. Do not present a grammatical explanation as a complete interpretation of an āyah.

# 4. Application Features

## MVP — Required

### A. Curriculum map
- Three modules, 26 weeks, chapters, lessons, and progress indicators.
- Show prerequisites and recommended next lessons.
- Allow learners to study out of order, but explain when a lesson depends on earlier concepts.
- Mark lessons as not started, in progress, completed, or needs review.

### B. Lesson player
- Responsive reading layout.
- Arabic text displayed right-to-left (`dir="rtl"`).
- English explanation displayed left-to-right.
- Support Arabic diacritics and correct shaping.
- Toggle between fully vowelled and unvowelled text only when both versions are available and verified.
- Reveal examples, rule cards, hints, and explanations progressively.

### C. Exercise engine
- Multiple choice.
- True/false.
- Matching.
- Fill in the blank.
- Word ordering.
- Conjugation table completion.
- Basic word-role and case identification.
- Immediate explanatory feedback.
- Retry incorrect questions.

### D. Progress and review
- Store progress locally in the browser.
- Track lesson completion, attempts, accuracy, and difficult concepts.
- Review missed questions.
- Basic spaced-review queue.
- Export/import progress as JSON so learners can back up or move their progress.

### E. Search and glossary
- Search Arabic grammar terms and English explanations.
- Glossary entries for terms such as *ism*, *fiʿl*, *ḥarf*, *rafʿ*, *naṣb*, *jarr*, *jazm*, *mubtadaʾ*, *khabar*, *fāʿil*, *mafʿūl*, and *iḍāfah*.
- Each glossary term links to lessons where it is taught.

## Later releases

- Full interactive iʿrāb annotation interface.
- Verb conjugation explorer for Forms I–X and irregular verb classes.
- Root and pattern explorer.
- Adaptive review based on learner performance.
- Guided Qur’anic and classical passage analysis.
- Teacher/mentor mode with shareable progress summaries.
- Optional audio for letter sounds and carefully verified recitation examples. Audio must not be treated as a substitute for qualified Qur’an recitation instruction.
- Installable Progressive Web App (PWA) and offline lesson access.

Do not build all advanced features before the lesson and exercise data model is stable.

# 5. Recommended Technical Architecture

## 5.1 Hosting and operating constraints

- Host the application as a static site on **GitHub Pages**.
- No mandatory backend, database server, paid API, or user account for the MVP.
- All core learning functionality must work in a normal modern browser.
- Keep the project portable: it should also run locally from a simple static web server.
- Use open-source dependencies only, and avoid dependencies where browser-native functionality is sufficient.

## 5.2 Suggested stack

- **HTML5** for semantic page structure.
- **Vanilla JavaScript (ES modules)** for application logic.
- **CSS3** for responsive layout and Arabic typography.
- **JSON** for curriculum, lessons, glossary, examples, and exercises.
- **localStorage** for the MVP’s progress and settings.
- **GitHub Pages + GitHub Actions** for deployment.
- Optional lightweight CSS framework only if it materially reduces work; do not introduce a large frontend framework for this static learning app.

Do not introduce React, Vue, Angular, a server-side database, or a build system without documenting a concrete requirement that the simpler architecture cannot meet.

# 6. Arabic Language and Typography Requirements

Arabic rendering is a core feature, not decoration.

- Use `lang="ar"` and `dir="rtl"` on Arabic content containers.
- Use `lang="en"` and `dir="ltr"` on English content.
- Use CSS logical properties (`margin-inline`, `padding-inline`, etc.) to support RTL and LTR layouts.
- Select a legible Arabic font with correct diacritic positioning and include licensing information if bundled.
- Test Arabic on desktop and mobile browsers.
- Ensure diacritics do not collide, clip, or become detached from letters.
- Do not use transliteration as a replacement for Arabic script.
- Transliteration may be shown as optional support for technical terminology, but Arabic script remains primary.
- Keep punctuation and mixed Arabic/English inline text readable; avoid forcing the whole page into RTL.

Example:

```html
<article class="lesson">
  <h2 lang="en" dir="ltr">The Nominal Sentence</h2>
  <p class="arabic-example" lang="ar" dir="rtl">
    الطَّالِبُ مُجْتَهِدٌ
  </p>
  <p lang="en" dir="ltr">The student is diligent.</p>
</article>
```

# 7. Content Quality, Sources, and Copyright

This project is inspired by the structure and grammar coverage of Haywood and Nahmad. It must not become an unauthorised reproduction of the book.

- Do not copy textbook paragraphs, tables, exercise questions, answer keys, or reading passages wholesale.
- Write original explanations, original practice questions, and original example sentences.
- Refer to chapter numbers and topics for navigation, but do not imply endorsement by the authors or publisher.
- Only include Qur’anic text, hadith, classical excerpts, translations, and commentary with clear source attribution and appropriate rights checks.
- Qur’anic Arabic text should be checked against a reliable verified text source.
- Hadith references must include collection and locator details where available; do not invent grading or attribution.
- Do not fabricate quotations or attribute paraphrases to a scholar.
- Separate grammar facts from interpretive or theological commentary.
- Have a qualified Classical Arabic reviewer check grammar, case endings, morphology, and translations.
- Record the source and reviewer status for each published content item.

Suggested content metadata:

```json
{
  "contentId": "example-001",
  "contentType": "original-example",
  "source": "Created for this application",
  "reviewStatus": "pending",
  "reviewedBy": null,
  "reviewedAt": null,
  "notes": "Verify vowel marks and grammatical analysis before release."
}
```

For sourced passages, replace the source value with a precise bibliographic or canonical reference. Never label unreviewed material as verified.
