To achieve deep reading comprehension of **Classical Arabic and the Qur’an**, modern communicative skills (speaking, casual dialogue, journalistic vocabulary, and spoken dialects) are completely discarded. 

Instead, this integrated curriculum synthesizes your primary instructional workhorse—**Haywood & Nahmad’s *A New Arabic Grammar of the Written Language*** (systematic pedagogy and translation exercises)—with your comprehensive reference manual—**Karin C. Ryding’s *A Reference Grammar of Modern Standard Arabic*** (rigorous morphosyntactic explanations, root-and-pattern phonotactics, and classical syntactic structures).

---

### Core Pedagogical Framework for Qur’anic & Classical Study
1. **Primary Dual Discipline (*Ṣarf* and *Naḥw*):** 
   * **Morphology (*Ṣarf*):** Word derivation, internal root vowelling (*wazn* / metric paradigms), and weak-radical morphophonemics.
   * **Syntax & Inflection (*Naḥw* and *Iʻrāb*):** Syntactic governance (*ʻamal*), syntactic operators (*ʻawāmil*), and short-vowel desinential markers (*rafʻ*, *naṣb*, *jarr*, *jazm*).
2. **Qur’anic Recitation & Full Desonantal Inflection:** In modern spoken Arabic or unvocalized reading, final inflection is lost in pause. For Qur’anic comprehension, every inflectional vowel (*ḥarakah*) must be vocalized because case markers dictate meaning (e.g., distinguishing the Agent/Subject from the Direct Object in inverted V-O-S Qur'anic structures).
3. **Dual Source Mapping:**
   * **Core Textbook & Exercises:** Haywood & Nahmad (Chapters 1–52; literary selections from the Qur'an, * Kalīlah wa-Dimnah*, and *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*).
   * **Deep Reference & Phonological Theory:** Ryding (Reference Chapters 1–39; morphological paradigms, case/mood syncretism, and syntactic operator analysis).

### **Week 1: Orthography, Syllable Quantities & The Zero-Copula Nominal Clause**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 1 & 2
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 2 (Phonology and Script, §§2.3–2.8) & Ch. 4 (Sentence Structures, §§4.1–4.2)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Naḥw* & Phonotactics):**
  * The 28 consonants; long and short vowels; auxiliary signs (*sukūn*, *shaddah*, *maddah*).
  * Distinction between *hamzat al-qaṭʻ* (phonemic/disjunctive) and *hamzat al-waṣl* (elidable/conjunctive) essential for Qur’anic reading.
  * The definite article (*al-*) and phonetic assimilation of sun letters (*ḥurūf shamsiyyah*) vs. moon letters (*qamariyyah*).
  * Structure of the equational nominal sentence (*al-jumlah al-ismiyyah*): Subject (*al-mubtadaʼ*) and Predicate (*al-khabar*); zero-copula syntax in the present tense.
* **Practice & Classical Application:** Reading vowelled text; parsing *mubtadaʼ* and *khabar* in simple equational clauses; **Haywood & Nahmad Exercises 1 & 2**.

  * Nunation (*tanwīn*) on indefinite nouns; accusative spelling convention with *alif*.
* **Practice & Classical Application:** Identifying case endings on definite and indefinite substantives; vocalizing governing prepositions; **Haywood & Nahmad Exercises 3, 4, 5 & 6**.

### **Week 3: Duals, Sound Plurals & Broken Plurals I**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 5 & 6

  * Broken plurals (*jamʻ al-taksīr*); the classical agreement rule: non-human plurals are grammatically treated as feminine singular.

### **Week 4: Advanced Broken Plurals & The Annexation Construct (*Al-Iḍāfah*)**

### **Week 5: Pronominal Enclitics & Demonstratives**

# Module 2: The Classical Verbal System, Mood Governance & Morphological Stems (*Ṣiyagh al-Fiʻl wa-Aḥwāluh*)
*Weeks 9–17: Imperfect moods (Indicative, Subjunctive, Jussive), Imperative, Passive Voice, Derived Triliteral Forms II–X, root phonotactics (doubled, hamzated, weak verbs), and root-based dictionary mechanics.*

### **Week 9: The Imperfect System & Subjunctive Governance (*Al-Nawāṣib*)**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 14 & 15
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 21 (§21.1) & Ch. 34 (Moods of the Verb: Indicative and Subjunctive)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Naḥw* & *Ṣarf*):**
  * The imperfect tense (*al-muḍāriʻ*): prefix and suffix conjugation paradigms; the indicative mood (*al-muḍāriʻ al-marfūʻ*) marked by *ḍammah* or retention of *nūn* in the "five verbs" (*al-afʻāl al-khamsah*).
  * Negation of past (*mā*) vs. present (*lā*).
  * The Subjunctive Mood (*al-muḍāriʻ al-manṣūb*): triggered by subordinating particles of purpose and intent (*an*, *lan*, *kay*, *li-*, *ḥattā*).
  * Subjunctive markers: *fatḥah* on singular stems; deletion of *nūn* (*ḥadhf al-nūn*) in duals and plurals; absolute future negation with *lan*.
* **Practice & Classical Application:** Translating clauses of purpose; parsing *lan* and *an* clauses in classical prose; **Haywood & Nahmad Exercises 25, 26, 27 & 28**.

### **Week 10: The Jussive (*Al-Jawāzim*), Imperative (*Al-Amr*) & Internal Passive (*Al-Majhūl*)**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 16, 17 & 18
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 35 (Moods of the Verb: Jussive and Imperative) & Ch. 38 (The Passive Voice)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Naḥw* & *Ṣarf*):**
  * The Jussive Mood (*al-muḍāriʻ al-majzūm*): governed by *lam* (past negation: *lam yafʻal* = "he did not do"), *lā al-nāhiyah* (prohibitive command), and conditional particles.
  * Jussive markers: *sukūn* on sound singulars; dropping of final *nūn*; apocopation (deletion) of final weak consonants.
  * Derivation of the Imperative (*fiʻl al-amr*) from the jussive base; prosthetic *hamzat al-waṣl* prefix and its vowelling (*ḍammah* vs. *kasrah*).
  * The internal/inflectional passive (*al-fiʻl al-mabnī li-l-majhūl*): vowel shifts (past *fuʻila*, imperfect *yufʻalu*).
  * Promotion of the direct object to "deputy agent" (*nāʼib al-fāʻil*), taking the nominative case (*rafʻ*).
* **Practice & Classical Application:** Converting active sentences to passive; constructing positive and negative commands; **Haywood & Nahmad Exercises 29, 30, 31, 32, 33 & 34**.

### **Week 11: Derived Verb Stems — Morphological Principles & Forms II, III, IV**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 19 & 20
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 20 (Verb Classes & Derivation Patterns, §§20.1–20.2) & Ch. 23–25 (Forms II, III, IV)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Ṣarf*):**
  * Systematic root augmentation (*al-mazīd*): semantic functions of prefixation, infixation, and consonant doubling.
  * Form II (*faʻʻala*): causative, intensive, declarative meanings; verbal noun (*maṣdar*) pattern *tafʻīl*.
  * Form III (*fāʻala*): reciprocal, associative, transitive effort; *maṣdar* patterns *mufāʻalah* and *fiʻāl*.
  * Form IV (*afʻala*): causative, transitive, entering a state/place; *maṣdar* pattern *ifʻāl*.
  * Predictable active (*mu- -i-*) and passive (*mu- -a-*) participles for derived forms.
* **Practice & Classical Application:** Deriving Forms II, III, and IV from basic triliteral roots; analyzing causative and transitive verbs in scripture; **Haywood & Nahmad Exercises 35 & 36**.

### **Week 12: Reflexive & Reciprocal Stems — Forms V & VI**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 21
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 26 (Form V) & Ch. 27 (Form VI)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Ṣarf*):**
  * Form V (*tafaʻʻala*): reflexive/resultative of Form II; taking on a quality or gradual process; *maṣdar* pattern *tafaʻʻul* (noting the stem vowel *ḍammah*).
  * Form VI (*tafāʻala*): mutual, reciprocal action among multiple subjects; feigning a quality; *maṣdar* pattern *tafāʻul*.
  * Syntactic shifts: transitives becoming reflexive/intransitive in Form V and reciprocal in Form VI.
* **Practice & Classical Application:** Identifying theological and spiritual vocabulary derived on Forms V and VI; **Haywood & Nahmad Exercises 37 & 38**.

### **Week 13: Passive-Reflexive & Participatory Stems — Forms VII & VIII**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 22
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 28 (Form VII) & Ch. 29 (Form VIII)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Ṣarf* & Phonotactics):**
  * Form VII (*infaʻala*): medio-passive, unaccusative (an action occurring to oneself without an external agent); *maṣdar* pattern *infiʻāl*.
  * Form VIII (*iftaʻala*): reflexive, participatory, intensive acquisition; *maṣdar* pattern *iftiʻāl*.
  * Form VIII dental assimilation rules: phonological shifts when the first radical is an emphatic consonant (*ṣād*, *ḍād*, *ṭāʼ*, *ẓāʼ*), a dental (*dāl*, *dhāl*, *zāy*), or a weak consonant (*wāw*).

### **Week 14: Stative & Inchoative Stems (Forms IX, X) & Doubled Verbs**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 23 & 24

* **Grammatical Focus (*Ṣarf*):**
  * Form IX (*ifʻalla*): bodily defects, colors, stative conditions.
  * Form X (*istafʻala*): requesting, seeking, or considering something to have a quality (*istifʻāl*, e.g., *istaghfara* = "to seek forgiveness"); *maṣdar* pattern *istifʻāl*.

* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 28, 29 & 33
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 22 (§§22.6–22.7, Hollow & Defective Roots) & Appendix I (How to Use an Arabic Dictionary)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Ṣarf*):**
  * Hollow Verbs (*al-fiʻl al-ajwaf*): middle radical *wāw* or *yāʼ* (e.g., *qāla*, *bāʻa*, *khāfa*).

### **Week 23: Diptote Declension (*Al-Mamnūʻ min al-Ṣarf*)**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 44
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 8 (§8.5, Diptotes: *Al-Mamnūʻ min al-Ṣarf*)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Naḥw*):**
  * Definition of diptotes: nouns barred from nunation (*mamnūʻ min al-tanwīn*) that take two case endings (nominative *ḍammah*, accusative/genitive *fatḥah*).
  * Morphological and semantic causes of diptote status:
    * Proper foreign names (e.g., *Ibrāhīm*, *Yūsuf*).
    * Feminine proper names, or names ending in augmented *alif-nūn* (*Sulaymān*).
    * Adjectives on the pattern *afʻal* (*akbar*, *aḥmar*).
    * Ultimate broken plural patterns (*ṣīghat muntahā al-jumūʻ*: *mafāʻil*, *mafāʻīl*).
  * The reversion rule: diptotes revert to full three-case triptote declension (taking a *kasrah* in the genitive) when prefixed by the definite article (*al-*) or when positioned as the first term of an *iḍāfah*.
* **Practice & Classical Application:** Vocalizing diptotes in nominative and genitive environments in scriptural text; **Haywood & Nahmad Exercises 81 & 82**.

### **Week 24: Comprehensive Accusative Syntax (*Al-Manṣūbāt*) & Apposition (*Al-Badal*)**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 45 & 46
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 8 (§8.3, Accusative Functions), Ch. 9 (§9.2, Apposition / *Badal*), & Ch. 11 (Adverbial Accusatives)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Naḥw*):**
  * Direct Object (*al-mafʻūl bihi*) and verbs taking two direct objects.
  * Absolute Cognate Accusative (*al-mafʻūl al-muṭlaq*): verbal noun used for confirmation, emphasis, or qualification.
  * Circumstantial Accusative of Manner (*al-ḥāl*): indefinite active/passive participle in the accusative describing the state of the subject or object during an action.
  * Accusative of Specification (*al-tamyīz*): clarifying ambiguous quantities, measurements, or comparative statements.
  * Accusative of Cause/Purpose (*al-mafʻūl li-ajlihi*).
  * The Permutative / Apposition (*al-badal*): identical replacement, part-of-whole, or quality; case agreement between *badal* and antecedent.
* **Practice & Classical Application:** Identifying and vocalizing *ḥāl*, *tamyīz*, and *mafʻūl muṭlaq* in Qur’anic prose; **Haywood & Nahmad Exercises 83, 84, 85 & 86**.

### **Week 25: Governing Particles, Corroboration (*Inna*) & Exception (*Al-Istithnāʼ*)**
* **Primary Curriculum:** Haywood & Nahmad Ch. 47, 48, 49, 50 & 51
* **Ryding Theoretical Reference:** Ryding Ch. 16 (Prepositions), Ch. 18 (Conjunctions), Ch. 19 (*Inna* and Her Sisters), & Ch. 37 (Negation & Exception)
* **Grammatical Focus (*Naḥw*):**
  * True prepositions (*ḥurūf al-jarr*) vs. locative/temporal semi-prepositions (*ẓurūf*).
  * Semantic nuances of coordinating conjunctions: *wa-* (simple link), *fa-* (immediate consequence), *thumma* (consequence with delay), *bal* (rectification).
  * Corroborating particles (*inna wa-akhawātuhā*): *inna* induces its subject into the accusative (*ism inna*) while its predicate remains nominative (*khabar inna*).
  * Syntax of Exception (*al-istithnāʼ*): syntax of *illā* across positive, complete negative, and defective negative clauses; non-particle exception words governing the genitive (*ghayr*, *siwā*).
* **Practice & Classical Application:** Parsing emphatic sentences introduced by *inna*; analyzing exceptions in theological covenants; **Haywood & Nahmad Exercises 87, 88, 89, 90, 91, 92, 93 & 94**.

### Textual *Iʻrāb* Parsing Routine for Daily Study
For every verse or classical passage read in this curriculum, execute the following three-step analysis:
1. **Sentence Boundary & Type:** Identify whether the clause is **Nominal (*Ismiyyah*)** (begins primarily with a noun/pronoun or *inna*) or **Verbal (*Fiʻliyyah*)** (begins with a verb).
2. **Isolate Nuclear Components:**
   * If Nominal: Locate the **Subject (*Mubtadaʼ*)** and **Predicate (*Khabar*)**, checking for copular modifiers (*kāna* or *inna*).
   * If Verbal: Locate the **Verb (*Fiʻl*)**, the **Agent (*Fāʻil*)**, and the **Direct Object (*Mafʻūl Bihi*)**.
3. **Account for Governing Agents (*ʻAwāmil*):**
   * Check if a verbal mood was transformed by *nawāṣib* (e.g., *an*, *lan*) or *jawāzim* (e.g., *lam*).
   * Check if a noun's case was altered by prepositions, *iḍāfah*, circumstantial status (*ḥāl*), specification (*tamyīz*), or exception (*illā*).
