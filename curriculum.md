To orient your studies exclusively toward **Classical Arabic, Qur’anic syntax, and classical literature**, the curriculum is restructured below. 

All modern conversational, journalistic, and spoken dialect components (such as Appendix A) have been stripped. The pedagogical focus shifts entirely to **Morphology (*Ṣarf*)**, **Syntax (*Naḥw*)**, **Grammatical Inflection (*Iʻrāb*)**, and **textual analysis of Qur’anic and classical excerpts**.

---

### Methodological Adaptations for Qur’anic & Classical Reading
1. **Reading Aloud with Full Inflection (*Iʻrāb*):** Modern spoken Arabic and casual reading drop case endings in pause; classical and Qur’anic recitation requires vocalizing every case and mood ending (*ḍammah*, *fatḥah*, *kasrah*, *sukūn*) to preserve grammatical meaning. Practice reading every exercise with full vowelling.
2. **Textual *Iʻrāb* Parsing:** For every exercise sentence, do not merely translate—identify the sentence type (Nominal vs. Verbal), the syntactic role of each word (Subject, Predicate, Agent, Object, Adverbial), and its governing factor (*ʻāmil*).
3. **Qur’anic & Classical References in the Textbook:** Utilize the textbook’s classical citations—such as Sūrat al-Fātiḥah (Ch. 52 supplement), Sūrat Yūsuf (Ch. 35, Ex. 56), classical traditions from *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, and the classical anthology guides in Appendix B.

# Module 1: Foundational Classical Morphology & Nominal Syntax (*Al-Mabādiʼ wa-l-Asmāʼ*)
*Chapters 1–13: Writing system, phonetics, nominal sentence structure, the three-case system, annexation (*Iḍāfah*), pronouns, and the Form I perfect verb.*

### **Week 1: Orthography, Syllable Structure & Equational Sentences**
* **Chapter 1: Orthography, Phonetics & Syllable Quantity**
  * **Classical Focus:** The 28 consonants; long/short vowels; orthographic signs (*sukūn*, *shaddah*, *maddah*); distinction between *hamzat al-qaṭʻ* (disjunctive) and *hamzat al-waṣl* (conjunctive) in Qur’anic recitation; syllable length and rhythm.
  * **Core Rules:** Phonetic assimilation of solar letters (*ḥurūf shamsiyyah*) vs. lunar letters (*qamariyyah*).
  * **Practice:** Reading vowelled classical syllables; tracking *hamzat al-waṣl* assimilation across connected phrases.
* **Chapter 2: The Definite Article & The Nominal Sentence (*Al-Jumlah al-Ismiyyah*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Structure of the equational clause: Inchoative/Subject (*al-mubtadaʼ*) and Enunciative/Predicate (*al-khabar*); zero-copula syntax in the present tense.
  * **Core Rules:** Indefiniteness indicated by nunation (*tanwīn*); definiteness through *al-* causing loss of nunation; subject-predicate gender/number agreement.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 1 & 2** (parsing nominal sentences; identifying definite subjects and indefinite predicates).

### **Week 2: Gender, Cases & Grammatical Inflection (*Iʻrāb*)**
* **Chapter 3: Gender & Collective Nouns of Unity**
  * **Classical Focus:** Real vs. grammatical feminine; nouns feminine by nature without markers; feminine marker *tāʼ marbūṭah* (ة); classical collective nouns (*ism al-jins*, e.g., *shajar* / trees) and their nouns of unity (*ism al-waḥdah*, *shajarah* / a single tree).
  * **Core Rules:** Adjectival agreement with feminine and collective nouns; interrogative particles (*a-* and *hal*) in classical queries.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 3 & 4** (identifying inherent feminines in classical prose; agreement drills).
* **Chapter 4: The Three Cases (*Aḥwāl al-Iʻrāb*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** The foundational threefold declension: Nominative (*marfūʻ* with *ḍammah* for subjects/predicates), Accusative (*manṣūb* with *fatḥah* for objects and adverbials), and Genitive (*majrūr* with *kasrah* after prepositions and in construct).
  * **Core Rules:** Orthographic addition of *alif* with accusative nunation (*-an*); governing prepositions.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 5 & 6** (vocalizing case endings on definite and indefinite nouns; identifying case functions in simple sentences).

### **Week 3: Duals, Sound Plurals & Broken Plurals I**
* **Chapter 5: Number — Duals, Sound Plurals & Personal Pronouns**
  * **Classical Focus:** Singular (*mufrad*), Dual (*muthannā* in *-āni / -ayni*), and Plural (*jamʻ*); sound masculine (*-ūna / -īna*) and feminine (*-ātun / -ātin*) plurals; independent personal pronouns (*ḍamāʼir munfaṣilah*).
  * **Core Rules:** Dual and sound plural case markers (inflection through letters rather than short vowels: *alif/yāʼ*, *wāw/yāʼ*); introductory V-S-O word order and subject-verb agreement.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 7 & 8** (inflecting duals and sound plurals across nominative, accusative, and genitive cases).
* **Chapter 6: The Triliteral Root System & Broken Plurals I (*Jamʻ al-Taksīr*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** The radical root system (*al-aṣl al-thulāthī*) and the metric paradigm based on *f-ʻ-l* (ف-ع-ل); the classical rule of non-human broken plurals (*jamʻ ghayr al-ʻāqil*) governed as feminine singular.
  * **Core Rules:** Root deduction; common broken plural patterns (*afʻāl*, *fuʻūl*, *fiʻāl*).
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 9 & 10** (extracting roots; pairing broken plurals with singular feminine adjectives).

### **Week 4: Broken Plurals II & The Genitive Construct (*Al-Iḍāfah*)**
* **Chapter 7: Advanced Broken Plurals & Collective Formations**
  * **Classical Focus:** Irregular broken plurals, quadriliteral root plurals (*faʻālil*, *faʻālīl*), and vocabulary frequent in classical discourse.
  * **Core Rules:** Metric pattern recognition; plural classification without reliance on English cognates.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 11 & 12** (pattern identification and dictionary lookup drills).
* **Chapter 8: The Genitive Construct (*Al-Iḍāfah*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** The annexation phrase: *al-muḍāf* (the possessed item, stripped of *tanwīn* and *al-*) and *al-muḍāf ilayh* (the possessor, strictly *majrūr* / genitive); multi-tier construct chains; metaphorical annexations in Qur’anic descriptions.
  * **Core Rules:** Case inflection of the *muḍāf* based on sentence role; preservation of the genitive in the *muḍāf ilayh*; dropping of dual and sound masculine plural final *nūn* in *iḍāfah*.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 13 & 14** (parsing chained constructs; translating classical possessive titles).

### **Week 5: Pronominal Clitics & Demonstratives**
* **Chapter 9: The Attached Pronouns (*Al-Ḍamāʼir al-Muttaṣilah*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Pronominal enclitics attached to nouns (signifying possession/genitive) and prepositions (pronominal objects).
  * **Core Rules:** Vowel shifts in third-person suffixes (*-hu / -him* shifting to *-hi / -him* following *kasrah* or *yāʼ*); prepositional idioms.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 15 & 16** (affixing enclitics to nouns and prepositions; case parsing of pronominal phrases).
* **Chapter 10: Demonstrative Pronouns (*Asmāʼ al-Ishārah*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Demonstratives for near (*hādhā / hādhihi*) and distant (*dhālika / tilka*) reference; classical dual and plural forms.
  * **Core Rules:** Syntactic difference between a demonstrative phrase (demonstrative + noun with *al-*, forming a noun phrase) and a demonstrative sentence (demonstrative + indefinite noun, forming complete equational clause).
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 17 & 18** (distinguishing attributive demonstratives from independent nominal clauses).

### **Week 6: Adjectival Syntax & Form I Perfect Verb (*Al-Fiʻl al-Māḍī*)**
* **Chapter 11: Descriptive Adjectives (*Al-Naʻt wa-l-Manʻūt*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** The fourfold concordance rule (gender, number, case, definiteness); diptote adjective patterns (*afʻal* for colors/defects, *faʻlān*); comparative and superlative elative patterns (*afʻal al-tafḍīl*).
  * **Core Rules:** Classical superlative constructs with genitive nouns; comparative clauses with *min*.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 19 & 20** (translating complex descriptive phrases; parsing superlative expressions).
* **Chapter 12: The Verb — Form I Perfect Tense (*Al-Māḍī*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Triliteral root verb conjugation; variations of the middle vowel (*faʻala*, *faʻila*, *faʻula*) indicating dynamic vs. stative actions; complete paradigm across 13 persons, genders, and numbers.
  * **Core Rules:** Verbal sentence word order (Verb-Subject-Object); agreement rules (verb remains singular when preceding an explicit plural subject).
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 21 & 22** (conjugating regular Form I verbs in the past; parsing classical V-S-O narratives).

### **Week 7: Pronominal Verb Objects & Defective Auxiliary Verbs (*Kāna*)**
* **Chapter 13: Pronominal Objects & *Kāna* and Its Sisters (*Kāna wa-Akhawātuhā*)**
  * **Core Grammar Rules:** Attaching object pronouns directly to verbs (*-nī*, *-ka*, *-hu*); the governing dynamics of *kāna* (past state): its subject (*ism kāna*) remains nominative (*marfūʻ*), while its predicate (*khabar kāna*) is placed in the accusative (*manṣūb*).

# Module 2: Verbal System, Mood Governance & Morphological Stems (*Ṣiyagh al-Fiʻl*)
*Chapters 14–33: Imperfect moods (Indicative, Subjunctive, Jussive), Imperative, Passive, Forms II–X, root phonology of doubled, hamzated, and weak verbs, and classical dictionary mechanics.*

### **Week 8: Imperfect System & Subjunctive Governance (*Al-Nawāṣib*)**
* **Chapter 14: The Imperfect Tense (*Al-Muḍāriʻ al-Marfūʻ*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Present and future continuous states; imperfect prefixes (*a-*, *ta-*, *ya-*, *na-*); indicative mood markers (final *ḍammah* or retained *nūn* in *afʻāl al-khamsah*).
  * **Core Rules:** Negation with *mā* (past) vs. *lā* (present); future particles (*sa-* and *sawfa*).
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 25 & 26** (conjugating Form I imperfect verbs; converting past narratives to ongoing descriptions).
* **Chapter 15: The Subjunctive Mood (*Al-Muḍāriʻ al-Manṣūb*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Subjunctive governing particles (*ḥurūf al-naṣb*: *an*, *lan*, *kay*, *li-*, *ḥattā*); absolute future negation with *lan*.
  * **Core Rules:** Subjunctive markers: final *fatḥah* on singular stems; apocopation/dropping of final *nūn* (*ḥadhf al-nūn*) in dual and plural paradigms.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 27 & 28** (translating subordinate clauses expressing purpose and intention; parsing *lan* clauses).

---

### **Week 9: Jussive Governance (*Al-Jawāzim*), Imperative & Passive (*Al-Majhūl*)**
* **Chapter 16: The Jussive Mood (*Al-Muḍāriʻ al-Majzūm*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Jussive triggers: past negation with *lam* (*lam yafʻal* = did not do); prohibitive commands with *lā al-nāhiyah*; introduction to conditional particles.
  * **Core Rules:** Jussive markers: final *sukūn* on sound singulars; dropping of final *nūn*; apocopation of final weak letters.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 29 & 30** (contrasting *mā* + perfect vs. *lam* + jussive; composing classical prohibitions).
* **Chapter 17: The Imperative (*Fiʻl al-Amr*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Derivation from the jussive stem; prefixing prosthetic *hamzat al-waṣl*; vowel quality of the prefix based on middle radical (takes *ḍammah* if middle vowel is *u*, *kasrah* if middle vowel is *a* or *i*).
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 31 & 32** (forming positive commands and pairing with *lā* + jussive negative commands).
* **Chapter 18: The Passive Voice (*Al-Fiʻl al-Majhūl*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Internal vowel transformations: perfect *fuʻila*, imperfect *yufʻalu*; syntactic deletion of the subject and promotion of the direct object to "deputy agent" (*nāʼib al-fāʻil*), taking the nominative case (*rafʻ*).
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 33 & 34** (converting active transitive clauses to passive; vocalizing *nāʼib al-fāʻil*).

### **Week 10: Derived Stems — Principles & Forms II, III, IV**
* **Chapter 19: The Extended Verb Paradigm (*Awzān al-Fiʻl al-Mazīd*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Semantic mechanics of root augmentation: how morphological additions systematically modify core root meanings; predictable generation of verbal nouns (*maṣādir*) and participles (*ism al-fāʻil / ism al-mafʻūl*).
  * **Practice:** Systematizing the derived verb chart across all ten stems.
* **Chapter 20: Forms II, III, and IV**
  * **Classical Focus:** Form II (*faʻʻala* — causativity, intensity), Form III (*fāʻala* — association, direction), Form IV (*afʻala* — transitive/causative, entrance into time/place); conjugations, participles, and *maṣdar* forms.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 35 & 36** (deriving Forms II–IV from triliteral roots; parsing causative vs. stative verbs).

### **Week 11: Reflexive & Reciprocal Stems — Forms V & VI**
* **Chapter 21: Forms V (*tafaʻʻala*) & VI (*tafāʻala*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Form V as reflexive/resultative of Form II; Form VI as mutual/reciprocal realization of Form III; distinctive *maṣdar* vowel patterns ending in *ḍammah* (*tafaʻʻul*, *tafāʻul*).
  * **Core Rules:** Syntactic shifts from transitive to intransitive/reflexive across stems.
  * **Practice:** **Exercises 37 & 38** (differentiating reciprocal dual/plural actions from transitive verbs; parsing reflexive theological vocabulary).

### **Week 12: Passive-Reflexive & Participatory Stems — Forms VII & VIII**
* **Chapter 22: Forms VII (*infaʻala*) & VIII (*iftaʻala*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Form VII as intrinsic passive/unaccusative (action occurring to oneself, never taking an explicit agent); Form VIII as subjective participation or intensive acquisition.
  * **Core Rules:** Form VIII dental assimilation rules: phonetic shifts when root initial consonant is *wāw*, *zāy*, *dāl*, *ṣād*, or *ṭāʼ* (e.g., *ittiḥād* from root *w-ḥ-d*).

### **Week 13: Stative & Desiderative Stems — Forms IX & X**
* **Chapter 23: Forms IX (*ifʻalla*) & X (*istafʻala*)**
  * **Classical Focus:** Form IX (fixed physical characteristics, bodily defects, colors); Form X (requesting, seeking, or considering something to possess a quality—*istifʻāl*, e.g., *istaghfara* / to seek forgiveness).
